## Product Overview
**One-liner:** replylane finds the X conversations that can become customers. You answer them.
**What it does:** A reply desk for X. You press Scan now and it gives you a short, ordered list of public posts worth replying to. Some are people asking to hire or buy (Asks). Some are recent posts from the ~100 accounts you chose to stay visible to (Room). Each card has a one-line reason it's there and, on Pro, a draft reply in your voice. You copy it and post it yourself. replylane never posts, likes, follows or DMs.
**Product category:** Reply desk / conversation desk for X. It sits next to the CRM and the calendar as a daily work object. It is not a publishing tool.
**Product type:** SaaS, web app (`dash.replylane.app`), magic-link sign-in.
**Business model:** Free plan, plus Pro at $29/mo billed through Whop. The packaging axis is Circle size (10 on Free, 150 on Pro).

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** One-person businesses and tiny teams whose pipeline comes from the founder's own presence on X. That covers B2B micro-SaaS, tools, productised agencies, specialist consultancies of 1–10 people, and creators with a product behind the audience.
**Decision-makers:** The founder or owner. They are also the user and the payer.
**Primary use case:** Answering "who should I reply to today?" in about eight minutes, then leaving X.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Catch the rare public "anyone know a good ___?" while the window is still open, without living in search.
- Stay familiar to the 50–150 accounts whose audiences overlap their buyers, even in bad weeks.
- See who they've gone quiet on before it shows up as a thin pipeline.
**Use cases:**
- A solo SaaS founder spots a "cheaper alternative to ___?" post 14 minutes after it went up.
- An agency owner comes out of a client week and checks the neglect list before anything else.
- A creator joins a two-week Channel around a launch spike without rebuilding a list.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** They use the For You feed as a CRM, and it fails in two ways. Buying intent dies before they see it, and relationships go quiet without anyone noticing.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **The feed:** it's ordered for attention, not for you. Asks surface forty replies deep.
- **X search:** it works if you live in it. Nobody lives in it.
- **Scheduling and publishing tools (Hypefury, Typefully, Buffer):** they handle what you post, not who you show up for.
- **Auto-repliers, pods and follow-farms:** they act on your account, sound like bots and taught the timeline to ignore strangers.
- **A regular CRM:** it doesn't know X exists.
**What it costs them:** Deals that went to whoever replied at 9:20. Relationships that cooled during a busy fortnight. Pipeline that thins weeks later with no obvious cause.
**Emotional tension:** A low hum of guilt about "should be more active." Fear of looking like a reply guy. The sting of "Haven't seen you around."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Nobody owns "who should I reply to today." The closest are AI reply tools (ReplyGuy-style). They fall short because they post or push volume, and their success metric is replies per day.
**Secondary:** X search, TweetDeck-style columns and lists. They fall short because they're manual and unranked, with no reason attached and no memory of who you've replied to.
**Indirect:** Scrolling the For You feed, or a VA or engagement pod. The feed is late and random. Pods and VAs damage trust and act on the account.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- **The Circle and neglect list.** "Who have I gone quiet on?" as a sorted answer, with Quiet checked before Neglected.
- **The weekly five with an anti-echo rule.** At least two of the five suggestions come from outside the current conversation.
- **A why-line on every card,** 18 words max. Nothing is scored without a reason.
- **Code-enforced draft rules.** No compliment openers, no pitch in sentence one, no pitch at all on Room posts, no links.
- **No write access, no send button, no background scanning.**
- **Separate filter bars.** Ask filters are never applied to your Circle.
**How we do it differently:** Fewer cards, better ones, and a human hits send.
**Why that's better:** Replies read as human because a human finished and sent them. The desk has a bottom, so the habit ends.
**Why customers choose us:** It's the only tool built around showing up for people rather than posting more. The moat is the Circle plus reply history, which gets more valuable the longer it's kept.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Isn't this another reply-guy tool?" | It can't post. There's no send button and there never will be. Drafts can't open with a compliment or pitch a Room post. The never list is enforced in code. |
| "Drafts will make me sound like a bot." | Three sentences max, no "Great question," no links or emoji. With no voice samples it writes plain and short instead of inventing a personality. You edit before you send. |
| "Asks will mostly be empty, so why pay?" | Correct, and we say so. Asks is the week-one proof. The Room is the daily value. Scan works on Free, so you find out before paying. |
| "I don't have time to curate 100 accounts." | Start with 5–10 you already read. Suggestions arrive to approve one at a time. If you won't curate at all, it isn't for you. |
| "Is my X account safe?" | Public posts, read-only. No X password, no write access, magic-link sign-in. |
| "Why not just search X myself?" | You can. The neglect list is the part search and the feed can't give you. |

**Anti-persona:** Paid-growth teams and ad-budget brands. SDRs counting leads. People who want to publish more. Anyone after auto-comment, pods or mass-follow. Anyone unwilling to curate ~100 accounts.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Seeing an ask forty replies deep. "Haven't seen you around." Watching the person who replied first get the work. Disgust with bot replies.
**Pull:** Eight minutes, then close the tab. A reason on every card. The neglect list. A tool that refuses to act on the account.
**Habit:** "I'll catch it in the feed." Scrolling feels like work. The belief that being active means posting more.
**Anxiety:** "Will I look like a reply guy?" "Will it touch my account?" "Is curating a Circle a chore?" "Another subscription that argues with me every month?"

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Dry, calm, slightly impatient with slop. A shop floor, not a launch party.
**Style:** Short sentences, specific nouns, concrete numbers over adjectives. Admits limits out loud. No exclamation marks.
**Personality:** Honest, restrained, specific, useful, a little stubborn.

## Goals

**Conversion action:** Start a desk (`https://dash.replylane.app/signin`).
